# Quizlet: study tools for solo learners

Research notes, checked against Quizlet's help center on 2026-11-12.

Quizlet is built around one object: the **flashcard set**, a list of term/definition (or question/answer) pairs. Almost every study tool is a different way of drilling that same set, and the newer AI tools exist mainly to turn raw material (notes, files, lecture audio) into sets and study guides.

Out of scope here: Study Groups, Study with Friends, Quizlet Live, Blast, classes/assignments, and Expert Solutions (premade textbook answers).

## 1. Creating study material

### Flashcard sets

- **What it does:** Holds your terms and definitions, with optional images and audio. Diagram sets (labelled images or maps) are a paid variant.
- **Purpose:** It is the single source every study mode reads from.
- **How it works:** You type cards into two columns and pick a language per side (including Chemistry and Math/Symbols). You can also import from a Word or spreadsheet file, or paste text. Quizlet suggests terms as you type, autosaves drafts, and makes published sets public by default.

### Smart Assist

- **What it does:** Generates draft flashcards from pasted text, an uploaded file, or just a topic or prompt.
- **Purpose:** Removes the tedious part of making a set.
- **How it works:** Cards appear in a preview state where you edit, delete, or add before publishing. You can switch between manual entry and Smart Assist within the same set.

### Study Guides

- **What it does:** Turns uploaded or pasted course material into a study guide with an outline and a linked flashcard set.
- **Purpose:** Gets you from messy notes to something structured and drillable in one step.
- **How it works:** Choose Generate → Study guide, add your notes, and Quizlet builds the guide. You can edit it afterwards, and it is saved in your library. It works in English, French, and German.

### Audio Notes

- **What it does:** Transcribes and summarises a lecture recording into a study guide and a flashcard set.
- **Purpose:** Lets you study from a lecture without writing notes yourself.
- **How it works:** You record in the iOS or Android app, or upload a file on the web (mp3, ogg, wav, m4a, aac; up to 5 hours or 500 MB). You select the audio language, and processing continues in the background. Free and Plus accounts get a limited number of recordings; the "Plus Unlimited" tier removes the cap.

## 2. Core study modes

### Flashcards

- **What it does:** Shows one card at a time; you flip it and move on.
- **Purpose:** Low-effort first exposure and quick review.
- **How it works:** Click to flip, and use the arrows to move. Options include shuffle, autoplay, text-to-speech, and choosing which side shows first. Turning on sorting splits cards into "Still learning" and "Know" piles. A round ends when you have seen every card.

### Spaced Repetition

- **What it does:** A scheduler inside Flashcards that decides when each card comes back. It launched in August 2026.
- **Purpose:** Long-term retention by reviewing each card just before you would forget it.
- **How it works:** After flipping a card you rate your recall, and the rating sets the next review:

| Rating | Meaning                    | Card returns      |
| ------ | -------------------------- | ----------------- |
| Repeat | Didn't remember            | Within minutes    |
| Hard   | Remembered with difficulty | Soon              |
| Okay   | Remembered                 | About a day       |
| Easy   | Remembered easily          | Four or more days |

- You review until no cards are due, and a "New cards per day" setting controls how much new material enters.
- Flashcards offers three study methods: Browse cards, Basic sorting, and Spaced repetition.
- It may be switched on automatically for larger sets, and you can get a daily reminder at 10 a.m.
- Keyboard: space flips, 1–4 rates.
- The help center says it is currently website-only.

### Learn

- **What it does:** Builds a personalised question sequence from a set, based on your goal and how well you know each term.
- **Purpose:** This is Quizlet's main "get me to mastery" mode; it focuses your time on what you haven't learned.
- **How it works:**
  - You open a set, select Learn, and choose a goal for the session.
  - Questions get progressively harder as you answer correctly, moving from recognition (multiple choice, true/false) to recall (written answers).
  - Options let you choose the answer side, question types, starred-only study, shuffle, audio, and a grading strictness level. Smart grading (paid) judges written answers on meaning instead of exact wording.
  - With written questions on, you can require retyping the correct answer after a miss.
  - Write and Spell, formerly separate modes, are now options inside Learn.
  - Answer Streaks start after five correct first-try answers in a row. Overriding a wrong mark with "I was correct" keeps the streak.
  - Subscribers also get custom study paths and progress tracking.

### Test

- **What it does:** Generates a one-off quiz from a set and grades it automatically.
- **Purpose:** A check on exam readiness, as opposed to Learn's ongoing practice.
- **How it works:** You set the number and types of questions (multiple choice, true/false, and others), optionally add a timer, then submit for a score and a review of your answers. You can restrict it to starred terms. Progress is not saved if you leave mid-test. You can print the graded test.

### Practice Tests

- **What it does:** Uses AI to write a full-length, exam-style test from your notes or flashcard sets.
- **Purpose:** Simulates a real exam, with questions that go beyond the literal term/definition pairs that Test reuses.
- **How it works:** Choose Generate → Practice test, pick sets or upload notes, then adjust question count, question type, and time limit. Quizlet says it currently works for science and humanities material, in English only. It cannot be generated from audio.

## 3. AI help and passive review

### Ask Quizlet

- **What it does:** A chat assistant that explains concepts step by step, finds relevant sets, and generates editable flashcards.
- **Purpose:** Keeps "I don't understand this" and "now let me practise it" in one place.
- **How it works:** You open it from the home page or a set page and type a question. You can attach up to three files (pptx, docx, pdf, png, jpg) for it to explain, summarise, or quiz you on. Suggested actions lead straight into a study session. Chat history is not saved once you leave. It is US-only, for ages 14 and up.

### Podcasts

- **What it does:** Generates a conversation between AI hosts about your material. It launched in August 2026.
- **Purpose:** Hands-free review while commuting or exercising.
- **How it works:** Choose Create → Podcast, then select flashcard sets, study guides, Google Drive files, or uploaded notes. An optional prompt steers the content. Quizlet does not correct errors in your source material.

## 4. Solo games

All three reuse a set's questions as the game mechanic, and their purpose is motivation: making a short study session easier to start.

- **Match:** You race a clock to pair terms with definitions, six pairs per game. Each wrong match adds one second.
- **Blocks:** You answer a question to earn three puzzle pieces, then place them on a grid to complete rows and columns for points.
- **Charms:** A match-three puzzle, new in August 2026. Matching a Question charm asks a question from your set, and a correct answer earns extra moves.

Blocks and Charms both need a set with at least six terms.

## How it fits together

1. **Capture:** Get material in by typing, importing, Smart Assist, Study Guides, or Audio Notes.
2. **Understand:** Read the study guide, or ask Ask Quizlet to explain.
3. **Memorise:** Use Flashcards first, then Learn for active recall.
4. **Retain:** Use Spaced Repetition for daily scheduled review.
5. **Check:** Take a Test or Practice Test before the exam.
6. **Fill gaps:** Use Podcasts and games for low-effort time.

## Not confirmed

- **Learn's internals:** Quizlet's help page is thin on this. The question-type progression comes from Quizlet's own older descriptions; the exact mastery rules and goal options are not published in the help center.
- **Q-Chat:** The earlier AI tutor no longer has a help-center page, and searching for it returns Ask Quizlet. It appears to have been replaced, but there is no official statement.
- **Pricing:** Quizlet's marketing pages blocked automated reading, and third-party sites disagree on Plus pricing, so prices are left out.
